Lane A: New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act

Issue: Does the pricing-label requirement in New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act apply to Home Depot's app prices for New York shoppers?

  1. RRule: What the law says

    When a business uses a shopper's personal data to set a price with an algorithm, it must show a clear label next to that price saying so.

  2. AApplication: Your guesses about Home Depot

    Guess: Home Depot's app personalizes some prices using shopper data, like browsing or purchase history.

    Guess: Those prices don't carry a label.

  3. CConclusion: The likely answer, as an "if"

    If Home Depot's app prices are personalized that way, the label rule applies, and a missing label would likely be a violation.

To confirm first: Whether app prices are set per shopper or are the same for everyone in an area. That one fact decides whether the law applies at all.

Lane B: Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA)

Issue: Does the written-consent requirement in Illinois' BIPA apply to Home Depot's self-checkout cameras in Illinois stores?

  1. RRule: What the law says

    Before collecting a face scan or other biometric data, a business must give written notice and get written consent.

  2. AApplication: Your guesses about Home Depot

    Guess: Self-checkout cameras identify shoppers' faces, not just record video.

    Guess: Shoppers aren't asked for written consent.

  3. CConclusion: The likely answer, as an "if"

    If the cameras identify faces without consent, Home Depot would likely be violating BIPA.

To confirm first: Both facts: what the cameras actually do, and whether shoppers get any notice or consent form.
