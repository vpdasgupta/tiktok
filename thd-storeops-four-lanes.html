Project 1 · Step 1

Pick your lane

Creating one unified store experience for The Home Depot is too big for anyone to cover alone. The work splits across four lanes — each a purposeful guardrail, narrow enough to keep you focused, open enough to be creative. You'll do all of Project 1 through the lens of the one you choose. Read each lane's question below and pick the one that pulls at you.

1

Customer Experience

The question you own

Where does the trip break as a customer moves between online, in-aisle, checkout, delivery, and pickup?

This lane is about friction — the moments a customer has to start over, wait, or do the store's stitching for it. Think Disney, where one identity carries your ticket, payments, and reservations so nothing resets, or Sephora, where an associate advises from your history. Both build a trip that never makes the customer backtrack.

2

Technology

The question you own

Where do the separate systems — mobile checkout, website, tool rental, delivery, inventory — fail to talk, so the customer's info doesn't follow them?

This lane is about the plumbing that makes a unified experience possible or impossible. The "endless aisle" only works if the inventory system, the ordering system, and the associate's device are actually connected. When systems don't talk, the customer feels it as "start over."

3

Workforce & Training

The question you own

When anyone can sell anywhere, what happens to roles, hierarchy, and what an associate needs to know to help outside their department?

This lane is about the people the tool empowers — and what they now need. Selling culture only works if an associate can confidently help a customer in an aisle that isn't theirs. The tool is only as good as the person holding it.

4

Asset Protection

The question you own

When anyone can sell anywhere, how do you know the customer paid — and keep people and products safe — without killing the smooth experience?

This lane lives on a real tension: frictionless vs. secure. The question is how to keep a checkout-anywhere store trustworthy when the basket is a flatbed, not a phone case — removing the register choke-point without inviting loss.
