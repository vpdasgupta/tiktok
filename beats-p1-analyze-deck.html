3.1 Analyze Your Repository

Turn 25 rows into 4–6 findings

A finding is something your data shows that Beats should care about.

  • 1 · CountRead the numbers your sheet already has
  • 2 · ComparePut Beats next to its competitors
  • 3 · So whatSay what the difference means for Beats

Where they go: Your findings become the Key Findings section of your Executive Summary in 3.3.

Description vs. analysis

Don't just report it. Explain it.

  • Description (what)"Beats used Celebrity/Influencer hooks in 6 of 10 ads."
  • Analysis (so what)"Beats used Celebrity/Influencer hooks in 6 of 10 ads, versus 2 of 15 for competitors. Beats is selling who wears the headphones, not how they sound."

Made-up numbers. Your findings will use your own data.

The finding formula

Number + comparison + so what

Number
6 of 10 Beats ads open on a famous face
Comparison
vs. 2 of 15 competitor ads
So what
Beats leans on fame to stop the scroll, while rivals lead with the product

Quick check: If your sentence has no comparison, it's still a description.

Step 1 · Count

Your Summary Dashboard already did the math

Open the Summary Dashboard tab. For every hook type, talent strategy, production level and platform, it shows:

  • CountHow many ads
  • % of adsShare of all the ads you logged
  • Avg effectivenessThe average of your 1–5 scores

Use it, don't rebuild it. The averages already answer "which hooks score highest?"

Step 2 · Compare

Split the counts by brand with a pivot table

The dashboard counts all brands together. A pivot table shows Beats next to everyone else.

  1. On the Ad Repository tab, click any cell in your data. Then choose Insert > Pivot table > New sheet > Create.
  2. Rows: click Add and pick Brand.
  3. Columns: click Add and pick Hook Type.
  4. Values: click Add and pick Ad ID. It counts your ads. Set "Show as" to "% of row".
  5. Rename the new tab "Hooks by Brand".

Then repeat with Talent Strategy in Columns, and name that tab "Talent by Brand".

Question 1 · Hooks

How does Beats grab attention?

  • Which hook does Beats use most? Which does it never use?
  • How does that compare with competitors?Your Hooks by Brand tab
  • Which hooks have the highest average score?Your Summary Dashboard

What Beats skips matters too. A brand that never uses Problem/Pain Point hooks is choosing not to sell headphones as a fix for a problem.

Question 2 · Talent and production

Who's in the ad, and how polished is it?

Look at the two together. Filter your sheet to one brand and read the Talent and Production columns side by side.

Question 3 · Platforms

Does each brand make ads for each platform?

  • Filter by Platform. Are the TikTok ads shorter or rougher than the YouTube ads?
  • Does any brand post the same polished ad everywhere?
  • Where does Beats fit?

Why it matters: In 1.1 you saw that TikTok favors ads that look like regular posts. A brand posting studio ads there may be missing attention.

Question 4 · Standout ads

What do your best ads have in common?

Sort the Ad Repository by Effectiveness Score, highest first. Pick your top 2–3 and say what made each one work.

Weak
"It works because it has a celebrity."
Strong (made up)
"The athlete is mid-workout before we see a logo, so it feels like real footage, not an ad. The bass drops right as the earbuds go in."

Name the specific choice, not the label. This becomes your Standout Ads finding.

Before you write

Three traps to avoid

  • Reading too much into small numbersYou have about 25 ads. A 4.2 vs. 4.1 average is a tie. Say "in my 4 Sony ads," not "Sony always."
  • Assuming one thing causes anotherCelebrity ads might score higher because they also have bigger budgets. Say "goes with," not "causes."
  • Stating the obvious"Headphone ads show headphones" isn't a finding. Skip it.
