3.1 Analyze Your Repository

Add 3 tabs to your Ad Repository

You'll do all of this in your own sheet. Click See the example at the top any time to see a finished version.

  • Hooks by BrandA pivot table of each brand's hooks
  • Talent by BrandThe same, for talent
  • Findings4–6 findings, one per row

A finding = number + comparison + so what. "6 of 10 Beats ads open with a famous face, vs. 2 of 15 competitor ads. Beats sells who wears the headphones." (made up)

Step 1 of 6

Build your Hooks by Brand tab

  1. On the Ad Repository tab, click any cell in your data.
  2. Choose Insert > Pivot table > New sheet > Create.
  3. In the panel on the right: Rows > Add > Brand. Columns > Add > Hook Type. Values > Add > Ad ID.
  4. Under Values, set "Show as" to "% of row".
  5. Double-click the new tab's name and rename it Hooks by Brand.

You're on track if… Each brand's row adds up to 100% in the Grand Total column.

If you're stuck… A hook nobody used won't get a column. That's fine, and it might be a finding.

Step 2 of 6

Build your Talent by Brand tab

Step 3 of 6

Set up your Findings tab

Click + at the bottom left of your sheet to add a tab. Name it Findings. In row 1, type these 4 headers:

Row 1 of your Findings tab
Row 1QuestionNumberComparisonSo what

You're on track if… Your Findings tab has these 4 headers in row 1.

If you're stuck… Choose View > Freeze > 1 row so the headers stay in place as you scroll.

Step 4 of 6

Write 1–2 hook findings

Open Hooks by Brand. Find Beats' biggest %, then compare it with the other brands. On the Summary Dashboard, check Avg effectiveness by Hook Type to see which hooks scored highest.

Choose Data > Create a filter. Filter Brand to Beats by Dre and read Talent (I) next to Production (M), then try a competitor. Next, filter Platform to TikTok, then YouTube, and compare Production (M) and Duration (E).

Step 6 of 6

Write a standout ad finding

Clear your filters. Click the filter icon on Effectiveness Score (N) and choose Sort Z → A. Look at your top 2–3 ads and name the specific choice that made each one work.

If you're stuck… Reread your Key Observations (O) for your top ads. They're a head start.

Before you submit

Check your findings for three traps

  • Reading too much into small numbersYou have about 25 ads. A 4.2 vs. 4.1 average is a tie. Say "in my 3 Sony ads," not "Sony always."
  • Assuming one thing causes anotherCelebrity ads might score higher because they also have bigger budgets. Say "goes with," not "causes."
  • Stating the obvious"Headphone ads show headphones" isn't a finding. Skip it.

Keep or drop: Compare with your 3 first patterns from 2.5. Keep the ones your data backs up.

How it will be evaluated

What strong analysis looks like

Two pivot tabs
Hooks by Brand and Talent by Brand, shown as % of row
4–6 findings
Covering hooks, talent and production, platforms and a standout ad
Complete rows
Every finding has a number, a comparison and a so what
Honest claims
Nothing bigger than your sample can support
