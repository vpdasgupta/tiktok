Challenge & Lane

The problem, in my words

DEP usually learns something in its water system has failed only after it breaks. Challenge 2 is an early-warning system — sensors and analytics that reveal the condition of pipes, equipment, and the water environment.

Why it matters to DEP

Every undetected leak wastes treated drinking water and risks a main break under a city street. Catching it early turns a dig-when-it-breaks operation into a predictive one — at the scale of 10 million people.

My lane — and why I chose it

Challenge 2 → Water supply & environmental field monitoring: startups making low-cost water-level sensors and co-located rain gauges a utility could deploy across green-infrastructure sites.

Maps to DEP's need: cloudburst / GI monitoring — low-cost, low-maintenance water-level sensors and rain gauges. Chosen because it's verifiable: a sensor has real deployments, or it doesn't.

🎤 Talking notes

What the scout would say to the panel while this slide is on screen. The slide stays tight; the detail lives here.

On “the problem”

DEP runs a billion-gallon-a-day water system across thousands of miles of mostly underground, often century-old infrastructure — and usually finds out something has failed only after it breaks. Challenge 2 is about giving DEP the ability to act before a break, an overflow, or a plant outage.

On “why it matters”

An undetected leak wastes treated drinking water and risks a main break under a city street; a green-infrastructure asset that quietly stops working means stormwater DEP thought it was managing is now flooding a neighborhood. Catching condition problems early is real money and real public-safety impact.

On “my lane”

I'm scouting water supply and environmental field monitoring companies — the low-cost sensors and rain gauges a utility could actually deploy at scale. It maps directly to DEP's cloudburst / GI monitoring need. Sourced globally, US-first. I chose it because it's concrete and verifiable, which makes the eligibility gate and relevance call cleaner to source from public evidence.

What makes this work

It's plain, it's specific, and it fits on one slide you could actually present. It names a use case, a specific DEP need, a concrete consequence, and an honest reason for the choice — not a vague preference. Your draft should do the same for your Lane.
