How to Build a Scoring Rubric

A rubric turns a gut feeling into a score anyone can repeat

  • Gut feeling"It just doesn't feel like Beats."
  • Rubric score"Feels real: 2. The star reads lines like a spokesperson, and no real fan appears."

The second one tells a creative team what to fix. That's the point of a rubric.

All examples in this deck are made up.

Pull your criteria from work you already did

Brand
From your Playbook: show or say the brand early

Sound leads
From your Standards, Sound: music leads the idea
Platform fit
From your Playbook: design for sound on and sound off

Use your Project 2 findings as a check. If real people said it mattered, it belongs in your rubric. (Example made up.)

Keep it short

5 criteria, one question each

  • HookDoes it stop the scroll in the first 3 seconds?
  • BrandIs Beats seen or heard by 5 seconds?
  • Feels realIs there a believable person or voice?
  • Sound leadsIs music or sound part of the idea?
  • Platform fitDoes it match the platform's format, length and captions?

Why 5? It covers what matters and you can still score an ad in a few minutes.

Scoring levels

Write what a 1, a 3 and a 5 look like

Use words for what you'd see or hear on screen.

Example (made up)
Hook: 1
Nothing in the first 3 seconds gives a reason to stay, like a slow logo or a still product shot.
Hook: 3
Something catches the eye, but it takes a few seconds to land.
Hook: 5
Movement, a face or a sound grabs you in the first 3 seconds.

2 and 4 mean "in between." You don't need to write them out.

Make it repeatable

Describe what you see, not how you feel

Vague words vs. words two people would agree on
Vague (avoid)Something you can see (use)
"Great hook"Movement or a face in the first 3 seconds
"Brand feels present"Logo or product on screen by 5 seconds
"Good energy"Cuts land on the beat of the music
"Feels authentic"A fan or creator speaks in their own words

Quick test: could two people watch the same ad and give it the same score?

Pass or fail

Set a pass rule with a floor

Example (made up)

Pass = 18 or more out of 25, and no criterion below 3.

  • Why a totalIt rewards ads that are strong across the board.
  • Why a floorA high total can hide one big weakness.

Example (made up): 5 + 4 + 2 + 5 + 3 = 19. That clears 18, but Feels real is a 2, so the ad fails.

Test it

Try it on ads you already know

  1. Score 2 Beats ads and 2 rival ads.
  2. Compare the scores with what you found in Projects 1 and 2.
  3. If a score surprises you, check your wording first.
  4. Fix the definition, then score again.

A rubric that agrees with your evidence is one you can trust on ads you haven't seen yet.

Watch out

3 rubric mistakes to avoid

  • Vague words"Good," "engaging," "premium." Swap in something you can see.
  • Two ideas in one"Hook and brand" can't get one score. Split them.
  • Every ad gets the same scoreIf all 4 ads get a 5, the criterion tells you nothing. Tighten it or swap it.
