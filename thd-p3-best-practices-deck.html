Know Your Smart Label Features & Best Practices: best practices

Best practice 1 of 6

Start where paper is slowest

Locked cases, cages, high shelves

Lever 3

What it does
puts smart labels first where each paper change takes the longest: find a key holder, unlock, swap, relock.
Price per item
none. It's a placement choice.
Lever
3 (seconds per change).
Proof
Home Depot already uses Chroma in the locked case at the front of the store. No public time study found.
Watch out
you need a believable time for a locked-case change. Ask MET, or label your number an estimate.

Best practice 2 of 6

Buy the cheapest size that fits

Lever 2

What it does
uses a small black-and-white label wherever the shelf doesn't need a bigger screen.
Price per item
small black-and-white about $5–$9, vs. $11–$17 for a 4.2-inch label [vendor].
Lever
2 (smart label price).
Proof
black-and-white is the cheapest option at every size [vendor].
Watch out
the price must stay readable, and unit-price rules may set a minimum text size.

Best practice 3 of 6

Batch price updates overnight; choose replaceable batteries

Lever 5

What it does
sends price changes in one batch instead of all day, so batteries last closer to their rating, and swaps batteries instead of whole labels.
Price per item
none for batching; a battery costs less than a label.
Lever
5 (running cost), and it protects the label's lifespan.
Proof
Chroma batteries are replaceable and rated up to 10 years, assuming about 2–3 updates a day [vendor]. Leroy Merlin's whole fleet wore out after about 8 years [independent].
Watch out
areas repriced daily drain batteries faster (MET partners).

Best practice 4 of 6

Put replacements and support in the contract

Levers 4 + 5

What it does
builds broken-label replacement and battery swaps into the yearly software fee, on top of Chroma's second-day support.
Price per item
part of the software fee; no published price.
Lever
4 and 5 (the cost of damage and upkeep).
Proof
some label software licenses cover "replacement labels, battery replacement at end of life" [vendor].
Watch out
it only counts if it's in writing.

Best practice 5 of 6

Check the signal before installing

No direct lever

What it does
tests coverage in the far corners of your area and adds a gateway where the signal is weak, before any label goes up.
Price per item
a one-time survey, plus extra gateways where needed (raises your gateway share a little).
Lever
none directly. It's what keeps your months real: the last whole-store test ended mostly because labels in remote areas couldn't be updated.
Proof
Chroma's communicator runs on its own radio, separate from Wi-Fi, and confirms each update [vendor]. A published store study needed gateways using more than one radio type to reach 98.5% coverage [independent].
Watch out
this is also your top risk mitigation on page 4.

Best practice 6 of 6

Reprice more often where prices move with a market

Lever 6

What it does
uses the labels to follow lumber, copper or online competitors more closely, so each label does more changes.
Price per item
none.
Lever
6 (price changes per product).
Proof
an academic study found that after stores added electronic labels, price changes became more frequent and smaller, with gains concentrated in fast-moving categories [independent].
Watch out
Walmart says it won't use its labels for surge pricing, and some states regulate dynamic pricing. More updates also drain batteries.
