Write Questions That Get Honest Answers

Small wording choices change the answers you get

A survey can give you clear answers to the wrong question. People also try to be polite, and they guess when a question is unclear.

Your job: make every question easy to answer honestly. Five rules help.

The rules

5 rules for honest answers

  1. Ask one thing at a time. "Creative and memorable" is two questions.
  2. Use neutral words. Leave out words like "exciting" or "annoying."
  3. Use everyday words. Say "the first few seconds," not "the hook."
  4. Use one 1–5 scale and label it. Don't switch to 1–10 halfway.
  5. Test before you launch. Two testers will find problems you can't see.

The rules in action

Weak questions and better ones

Before and after: 4 common wording problems
ProblemWeakBetter
Two in one"How creative and engaging was the ad?""How creative was the ad?" and "How engaging was the ad?"
Leading"How much did you love this exciting ad?""How much did you like this ad?"
Jargon"Rate the hook's effectiveness.""How much did the first few seconds grab your attention?"
Extreme"This is the best ad I've ever seen.""This ad was easy to remember."

Your survey

Every survey in this project has the same shape

Everyone uses the same core questions, so results are easy to compare.

  1. Intro: it's anonymous, takes about 8 minutes and is for a student project.
  2. Screener: "Are you 18 or older?" No ends the form.
  3. One section per ad, 4 times: the same block of questions.
  4. Compare: 3 questions about all 4 ads.
  5. About you: age range and the platform used most.
  6. Your own: 1–2 extra questions, if a research question needs them.

Grid scale
5 points, Strongly disagree to Strongly agree
Optional
"What stood out to you, good or bad?"

Why: in Step 3 you line up all 4 ads on the same 5 numbers.

Your survey

Three questions that compare all 4 ads

Multiple choice. The 4 ads are the options.

  1. Which ad was the most memorable?
  2. Which was the most believable?
  3. Which would most make you want to buy?

Tip: label options the way people saw them, such as "Ad 1 (the athlete ad)," so they don't have to scroll back.

Your survey

Open-ended questions give you quotes

"What stood out to you, good or bad?" lets people answer in their own words. "Good or bad" tells them either answer is fine.

  • Keep it optionalRequired essays make people quit.
  • Use it in Step 4One clear quote next to a number makes a finding stronger.

Quality checks

A screener and an attention check

  • Screener"Are you 18 or older?" If someone picks No, the form ends. This keeps your survey to adults.
  • Attention checkOne extra row in one ad's grid: "For this row, pick Agree." Anyone who picks something else wasn't reading.

In Step 3 you'll drop responses that fail the attention check or give the same answer on every row.

Before you build

Revision checklist

  1. Does each question ask only one thing?
  2. Could someone give any answer without feeling judged?
  3. Would a 16-year-old understand every word?
  4. Do you know which research question each one serves?
  5. Does the whole survey take about 8 minutes?

Before you build

Ask AI to check your wording for bias

Paste this prompt into an AI tool such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, with your 1–2 extra questions and any wording you changed.

Prompt to copy

I'm a student building a short survey about 4 headphone video ads. Please review my questions for: 1. Leading or loaded words 2. Questions that ask two things at once 3. Words a 16-year-old might not understand 4. Answer options that are missing or overlap Here are my questions and answer options: [paste them]. For each problem, quote the question and suggest a neutral rewrite. Don't add new questions.

You decide. Keep the core questions as they are, so your results line up with everyone else's.
