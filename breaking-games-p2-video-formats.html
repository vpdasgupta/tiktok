Video formats

A format is a shape viewers already know

A format is a repeatable shape for a video, like a tutorial or a "get ready with me." Viewers recognize it in the first second, so they know what they're about to get.

Format: the shape of the story

Play-Along, Tutorial, Get Ready With Me, POV. You pick it from your player's moment.

Filming style: how you shoot it

Voiceover over your hands, a slideshow, or the camera as your eyes. You pick it when you plan.

Same game, different format, different video. Caminito as a Play-Along is a guessing game. As a Tutorial, it's a 20-second lesson.

Play-Along

Viewers play with you: they guess, pick a side or answer before the reveal.

Hook
Ask a question viewers can answer in the comments.
5 shots
1 the hook question, game in frame · 2 and 3 the play that holds the clue · 4 the reveal: box and name on screen · 5 end card
Best for
Danny. He comments his answer, and it doesn't feel like an ad.
Try it
"Guess this game from one move?" with one Caminito card face down.

Tutorial

Viewers learn something useful, fast: how to play, how to set up, one winning move.

Hook
Promise something useful, with a time on it.
5 shots
1 the promise · 2 to 4 one step per shot · 5 the result, then the end card
Best for
Rachel. She has no patience for a long rulebook.
Try it
"Rainy Saturday? Learn The Game of 49 in 20 seconds."

Get Ready With Me: game night edition

A "get ready with me" (GRWM) walks viewers through getting ready for something. Here, it's getting ready for game night.

Hook
Name the night and what's at stake.
5 shots
1 the hook: who's coming and when · 2 snacks and the table · 3 picking the game, box in hand · 4 the table ready, first card dealt · 5 end card
Best for
Danny's Friday game night. It works for Rachel's family birthday too.
Try it
"GRWM: hosting game night for 8 people who've never met."

Most GRWMs are filmed face to camera. Hands and a voiceover work just as well, and keep you off camera.

Day-in-the-Life

Follow one real moment from start to finish, like a mini vlog.

Hook
Feel close and real: drop viewers into the moment.
5 shots
1 the moment · 2 and 3 key moments · 4 the "good game" moment · 5 end card
Best for
Danny. He wants to see how a game night actually goes.
Try it
"Friday game night, 8 people, half strangers."

POV

POV (point of view) puts the viewer inside the moment: "POV: you ___." The camera is their eyes.

Hook
On-screen text that starts with "POV:" over the scene.
5 shots
1 the "POV:" text over the scene · 2 and 3 what happens, from the viewer's eyes · 4 the payoff · 5 end card
Best for
Danny. It lets him picture himself at the table.
Try it
"POV: you brought the game that saved the party." Film with the phone at chest height.

Unboxing and First Play

Open a game for the first time: what's inside, then the very first move.

Hook
The sealed box in frame and a question, like "What's actually in the box?"
5 shots
1 the sealed box and the hook · 2 what's inside · 3 the first move · 4 your verdict in one line · 5 end card
Best for
Rachel. She buys games as gifts and wants to see what she's getting.
Try it
"Gift check: what's actually in the box?"

Top 3 List

Three picks for one moment, one game per shot.

Hook
Name the moment and the number.
5 shots
1 "3 games for ___" · 2 to 4 one game per shot: the box and a one-line reason · 5 end card
Best for
Rachel, at gift time or before a family visit.
Try it
"3 games the grandparents can play too."

Trends: ride one with any format

A trend is a sound or a joke format that's everywhere for a few weeks. Any format above can borrow one: use the trend, then twist it toward the game.

✅ Use

Family-safe trends that let the game be the star. Credit the creator if you borrow their idea.

🚫 Skip

Adult jokes, pranks on people, or anything that mocks someone.

Trends fade in weeks. The formats on these slides last, so start with one of them.

The Op case: real play beats polish

The Op, another game company, got about 8 million TikTok views in one month. Most viewers didn't follow the account.

  • Low-polish phone footage of real play, not studio ads.
  • About 80% entertaining posts to 20% promoting.
  • For you: pick a format that shows real hands at a real table, and entertain first. Promote rarely.

Check yourself: which format fits?

1. Rachel wants a game the grandparents can learn tonight.

Tutorial (or a Top 3 List). She needs to see it's quick to learn before she'll try it.

2. You want Danny to comment, not just watch.

Play-Along. A guess gives him a reason to comment, and comments are a signal TikTok tests for.

3. Danny is hosting people who don't know each other on Friday.

Get Ready With Me or POV. Both drop him into the night he's planning.
