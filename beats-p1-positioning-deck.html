3.2 Compare Competitive Positioning

Add a Positioning tab to your sheet

Positioning is the spot a brand owns in buyers' minds. Every choice in its ads shows it.

  • Brand profilesOne row per brand
  • A 2×2 mapWhere each brand sits
  • Rival, threat and openingThree lines about Beats

Where it goes: Section 4, Competitive Positioning, of your Executive Summary.

Step 1 of 5

Set up your profile table

Add a tab named Positioning. In row 1, type these 6 headers. In column A, list Beats, Apple AirPods, Sony and Bose, plus any brand you logged 3 or more ads for.

Row 1 of your Positioning tab
Row 1BrandTop hookTop talentUsual productionAvg scoreTheir ads say…

You're on track if… You have 6 headers and at least 4 brands.

If you're stuck… Not sure a brand has 3 ads? Check By Brand on your Summary Dashboard.

Step 2 of 5

Fill in each brand's row

Top hook
The biggest % in that brand's row on Hooks by Brand
Top talent
The biggest % on Talent by Brand
Usual production
Filter the Ad Repository to the brand. Which Production Level (M) shows up most?
Avg score
With the filter on, select the brand's scores in column N. Click the Sum at the bottom right and pick Average.

You're on track if… Every cell is filled except Their ads say…

If you're stuck… Two options tied? Write both.

Step 3 of 5

Write what each brand's ads say

In the last column, write one short line on what the brand wants you to believe. Base it on the ads you logged, not on what you already knew.

Sample (made up)

Sony: "Nothing sounds better or blocks more noise."

Beats: "Wear what the stars wear."

You're on track if… Each line fits only that one brand.

If you're stuck… Read the brand's top hook and top talent together. What do they promise?

Step 4 of 5

Build your 2×2 map

A few rows below your table, make a 2×2 grid. Label the columns Sound and tech and Style and culture, and the rows Premium and Everyday. Type each brand into one box.

Sample (made up)
PriceSound and techStyle and culture
PremiumSony, Bose, Apple AirPodsBeats
Everyday(empty)Skullcandy

You're on track if… Every brand in your table sits in exactly one box.

If you're stuck… Problem/Pain Point hooks and product-only ads point to sound and tech. Celebrity and creator talent point to style and culture. Studio production points to premium; UGC style points to everyday.

Step 5 of 5

Name Beats' rival, threat and opening

Under your map, write 3 lines. Each one needs a box on your map or a number from your sheet behind it.

Sample (made up)
Closest rival
Skullcandy, the only other style-and-culture brand, one box down at a lower price.
Threat
Skullcandy's creator ads averaged 4.1, close to Beats' 4.2. Beats' style edge may be easier to copy than it looks.
Opening
None of my 25 ads used a Question hook. Beats could test one to stand out in the feed.

You're on track if… A reader could check each line against your sheet.

If you're stuck… Closest rival = the brand in Beats' box or the nearest one. An empty box can be an opening.
