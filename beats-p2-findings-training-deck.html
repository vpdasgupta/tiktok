4.1 From Numbers to Findings

Your Results tab says what happened. Findings say what it means.

  • ResultBeats_YouTube_01 scored 3.0 on believable.
  • FindingIt scored lowest on believable, 1.2 below Skullcandy_TikTok_01. Stars may get attention without trust.

All numbers in this deck are made up. They come from a 12-response example survey.

Finding vs. insight

A finding is what you saw. An insight is why it matters.

The format

Number + comparison + so what

You used this in Project 1. Use it again. Every finding needs all 3 parts.

Number
Skullcandy_TikTok_01 scored 4.2 on believable, and 6 of 12 picked it as most believable
Comparison
vs. 3.0 for Beats_YouTube_01
So what
A creator talking to camera earned more trust than a star.

Missing a part? No number means it's an opinion. No comparison means nobody can tell if it's high. No so what means nobody knows why it matters.

The so-what ladder

Keep asking "so what?" until you reach Beats

  1. Finding: Beats_YouTube_01 scored 4.5 on attention but 3.0 on believable.
  2. So what? People noticed it but didn't buy the message.
  3. So what? 8 of 12 said it felt like an ad. The star made it feel paid for.
  4. So what for Beats? Beats could pair the star with a real fan.

Stop at Beats. Two or three rungs is enough. If the last rung could apply to any brand, climb back down one.

How sure are you?

Backed up, challenged or one source

  • Backed upA second source agrees. Example (made up): 6 of 10 comments on Beats_YouTube_01 were about the athlete, not the headphones.
  • ChallengedA second source disagrees. Keep it and say so. That's useful too.
  • One sourceOnly your survey shows it. That's fine. Just label it.

Your second source is the optional comment check from 3.3. Skipped it? Mark your findings "One source."

How you say it

Confident on facts, careful on what they mean

Match your words to what you know
You're statingSound like thisNot this
A fact"Beats_YouTube_01 scored 3.0 on believable.""It may have scored around 3."
A meaning"In my 12 responses, this suggests stars get attention, not trust.""This proves celebrities don't work."

Under 30 responses? Call them early signals. Words like "suggests" and "in my sample" keep you honest.

The "Beats could…" line

Weak vs. strong ideas for Beats

Make it something a creative team could try next week
WeakStrong (made-up example)
Beats could be more authentic.Beats could pair the star with a real fan who says why they wear them.
Beats could use TikTok more.Beats could test 2 creator-led TikToks against 1 star-led one.
Beats could talk about the product.Beats could put one product fact inside a story, like battery life on a long run.

Avoid these

The 3 insight mistakes

  • Restating the data"Beats scored 3.0 on believable" is a number, not a finding. Add the comparison and the so what.
  • Too generic"Authenticity matters" fits any brand. Name the ad, the score and Beats.
  • Too big a leap"Beats should drop celebrities" goes past 12 responses. Suggest a test instead.
