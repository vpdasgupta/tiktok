Free-Tools Key Terms

The words you’ll meet while reading a company on Crunchbase and checking ownership on SEC EDGAR. Skim them once, then keep this open while you profile.

Crunchbase: Funding & Stage

How money raised and round names tell you how far along a company is.

  • Funding round

    One event where a company raises money from investors. Rounds have names that roughly track how early the company is.

    Why a scout cares: The round name is a one-word read on stage — earlier round, earlier company.

  • Seed round

    Usually a company’s first meaningful outside funding — very early stage.

    Why a scout cares: A Seed company is early and scrappy, right in the early-to-growth range the challenge wants.

  • Series A

    Typically the next major round after seed, once a company has some traction and is scaling.

    Why a scout cares: Signals a company that’s proving itself — still early, but past the first scrappy stage.

  • Paywall

    A field or page a site blocks unless you pay for a subscription.

    Why a scout cares: A blurred field is “not free,” not “not disclosed.” Say “not confirmed from free sources” rather than guessing what’s behind it.

SEC EDGAR: Ownership & Public vs. Private

Who owns whom — and the fastest free way to tell if a company can qualify.

  • Private company

    Owned by its founders, employees, and private investors; its shares are not traded on a public stock market.

    Why a scout cares: Our rules require a candidate to be independent and privately held — private is the passing state.

  • Public company

    Its shares trade on a stock exchange (like the New York Stock Exchange), so anyone can buy them. Public companies must file regular financial reports with the U.S. government.

    Why a scout cares: A public company — or a subsidiary of one — fails the eligibility gate.

  • Parent company

    A company that owns another company.

    Why a scout cares: If your find has a parent, the parent is who would be filing — so it’s the parent you check for public/private.

  • Subsidiary

    A company owned by a parent. If the parent is public, the subsidiary is a public subsidiary — and, for us, ineligible.

    Why a scout cares: A subsidiary of a public parent fails eligibility no matter how good the product is. Spotting one saves wasted work.

  • SEC

    The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, the government agency that regulates public companies.

    Why a scout cares: The SEC runs EDGAR — the free database that settles whether a company is public.

  • EDGAR

    The SEC’s free public database of the documents (“filings”) that public companies are required to submit.

    Why a scout cares: Filings there mean public; no filings means likely private (with a caveat for foreign parents).

  • 10-K

    A public company’s required annual report.

    Why a scout cares: Find a 10-K on EDGAR and that entity is public — a fast, definitive read.

  • S-1

    What a company files when it’s about to sell shares to the public for the first time.

    Why a scout cares: An S-1 means the company is public, or about to be.

  • Ticker

    The short symbol a stock trades under (for example, ROK for Rockwell Automation).

    Why a scout cares: Record the ticker as your evidence that a company or its parent is publicly traded.
