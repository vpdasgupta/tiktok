Five Ways a Label Gets Damaged

A warehouse doesn't threaten a label in one way — it threatens it in five. Forklifts are the one everyone pictures first, but they're only one of five. Learn all five, and you have a checklist you can run against any bay in the store.

  • 1🚜 Forklift & equipment strikes

    Forks, pallet jacks, and pallets swing wide and clip anything on the beam in their path.

  • 2📦 Falling product & equipment

    Boxes, lumber, and tools slide or tip off shelves and crush a label below.

  • 3🖐️ Customer & associate contact

    Everyday bumps, grabs, and scrapes from shoppers and restockers add up.

  • 4🔋 Malfunctions & failure

    No impact needed: a dead battery, dropped connection, or frozen screen shows the wrong price.

  • 5🌦️ Outdoor / weather exposure

    Garden and lumber areas add heat, cold, rain, and dust that stress screen and battery.

Threat 1 of 5

1

Forklift & equipment strikes

Forks, pallet jacks, and pallets swing wide when moving product on and off racking. Anything on the beam in the path gets clipped.

Threat 2 of 5

2

Falling product & heavy equipment

Boxes, lumber, and tools slide off pallets, tip off high shelves, or get set down hard — crushing or cracking a label below or beside them.

Threat 3 of 5

3

Customer & associate contact

Shoppers bump, grab, and lean on labels; associates knock or scrape them while restocking. Constant, everyday, low-grade contact adds up.

Threat 4 of 5

4

Malfunctions & technical failure

Even with no impact at all, a label can quit: a drained battery, a dropped connection, a frozen screen, or dust and moisture in the electronics. A dead-but-unbroken label still shows the wrong price.

Threat 5 of 5

5

Outdoor / weather exposure

Garden, lumber yards, and covered outdoor areas add heat, cold, rain, and dust that stress a screen and its battery.
