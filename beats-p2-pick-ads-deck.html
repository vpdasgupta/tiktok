1.3 Pick Your Ads and Questions

Add a P2 Survey Plan tab to your Ad Repository

You'll do all of this in your Project 1 Ad Repository.

  • 4 ads2 Beats and 2 rivals, plus 1 backup
  • 3 research questionsEach tied to a Project 1 pattern
  • 2 datesWhen you launch and when you close

Why a tab: in Step 2 you'll build your survey straight from it.

Step 1 of 5

Add the tab and the ad headers

  1. Open your Ad Repository.
  2. Click + at the bottom left to add a tab.
  3. Double-click the new tab's name and type P2 Survey Plan.
  4. In row 1, type 4 headers: Ad ID, Brand, Link, What it tests.

You're on track if… You have a tab named P2 Survey Plan with 4 headers in row 1.

If you're stuck… Use the exact tab name. Your program manager will look for it.

Step 2 of 5

Pick your 4 ads and a backup

Filter your Ad Repository tab by Brand. Pick 2 Beats ads and 2 rival ads that differ in something you want to test. Copy their Ad ID, Brand and Link into rows 2–5. Add a backup in row 6.

Sample (made up), Link column left out
Ad IDBrandWhat it tests
Beats_YouTube_01Beats by DreAthlete/celebrity hook
Beats_TikTok_01Beats by DreCreator-led TikTok
Skullcandy_TikTok_01SkullcandyA rival's creator-led TikTok
Sony_YouTube_01SonyProduct and tech features

You're on track if… Each ad is under 60 seconds, and each link works.

If you're stuck… No YouTube version? TikTok or Instagram is fine.

Step 3 of 5

Write 3 research questions

In row 8, add 3 headers: #, Research question, P1 pattern it tests. Base each question on a different finding.

Sample (made up)
#Research questionP1 pattern it tests
1Do celebrity hooks make Beats ads more believable, or just more noticeable?6 of 10 Beats ads open with a star; 2 of 15 rival ads do.
2Is a creator-led TikTok more believable than a celebrity ad?Skullcandy uses creators in all its ads.
3Are tech-focused ads as easy to remember as people-led ads?Rivals lead with the product. Beats leads with people.

You're on track if… Each question tests one thing and names a comparison.

If you're stuck… Try: "Does [P1 pattern] beat [another approach] with real people?"

Step 4 of 5

Pressure-test your questions with AI

Paste this prompt into an AI tool such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Fill in the brackets first.

Prompt to copy

I'm a student running a short survey (about 15 people) on 4 headphone video ads: [list your 4 ads and what each one tests]. For each ad, the survey asks how much people liked it (1–5) and how much they agree it grabs attention, is easy to remember, is believable and makes them want to learn more (1–5). Then it asks which ad was the most memorable, the most believable and the one that would most make them want to buy. Here are my 3 research questions: [paste them]. For each question, tell me: 1. Can this survey answer it? If not, what's missing? 2. Does it test only one thing? 3. Does it name a clear comparison? 4. Is the wording neutral? Suggest one short rewrite if a question needs it.

You're on track if… You kept or changed each question on purpose, and you can say why.

If you're stuck… AI may suggest bigger questions. Keep yours small enough for a survey of about 15 people.

Step 5 of 5

Set your launch and close dates

Under your questions, add two rows: Launch date and Close date. Launch at the end of Step 2 and close about 4 days later. Check sharing is still "Anyone with the link can view."

Sample (made up)
Launch date
Thursday, after I test my survey
Close date
Monday, or at 15 responses

You're on track if… Your close date gives people about 4 days to answer.

If you're stuck… Short on time? Launch sooner. A late launch cuts the time people have to answer.
