Every good issue fits one pattern

An issue is one question that connects one thing the law requires to one thing Home Depot does.

Does 1[one requirement] in 2[your law] apply to Home Depot's 3[specific practice]?

  • 1One requirement

    Just one thing the law asks a business to do, not the whole law. For example: get consent, show a label, or delete data after a set time.

    Find it: the "What it requires" column of your map.

  • 2Your law

    The law's exact name, so anyone reading your question can look it up. Not a lane or a topic, like "biometrics."

    Find it: the law you picked in Step 1, named just as it is on your map.

  • 3A specific practice

    One thing a big retailer like Home Depot plausibly does: an app, a camera, a time clock, a loyalty program.

    Find it: your own thinking about how Home Depot runs. It's a guess until you confirm it.

Lane A: Pricing · One law, three issues

2 of 3

New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act

What it requires: When a business uses a shopper's personal data to set a price with an algorithm, it must show a clear label next to that price saying so.

Not an issue yet: Surveillance pricing is a whole area of law. New York's pricing law and Home Depot names a law, but not which part or what Home Depot does.

  1. Issue 1

    Does 1the pricing-label requirement in 2New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act apply to Home Depot's 3app prices that change based on a New York shopper's browsing history?

  2. Issue 2

    Does 1the pricing-label requirement in 2New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act apply to Home Depot's 3personalized Pro Xtra discount offers sent to New York members?

  3. Issue 3

    Does 1the label-placement rule in 2New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act apply to Home Depot's 3personalized prices in website search results?

Lane B: Biometrics · One law, three issues

3 of 3

Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA)

What it requires: Before collecting fingerprints, face scans, and similar data, a business must give written notice and get written consent. It also needs a public policy for how long it keeps that data, and can't share it without consent.

Not an issue yet: Biometrics is a whole area of law. Illinois' biometric privacy law and Home Depot names a law, but not which part or what Home Depot does.

  1. Issue 1

    Does 1the written-consent requirement in 2Illinois' BIPA apply to Home Depot's 3fingerprint time clocks for hourly workers in Illinois stores?

  2. Issue 2

    Does 1the public data-retention policy requirement in 2Illinois' BIPA apply to Home Depot's 3self-checkout cameras that capture shoppers' faces in Illinois stores?

  3. Issue 3

    Does 1the ban on sharing biometric data without consent in 2Illinois' BIPA apply to Home Depot's 3use of an outside vendor to run face matching on store camera footage?
