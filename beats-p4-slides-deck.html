Build Your Presentation Slides

Build your deck from your Presentation Story

Every row in your slide plan becomes one slide. Click See the example at the top any time to see a finished 8-slide deck.

File
Google Slides, named Beats_Creative_Strategy_Presentation_<YourName>
Length
6–10 slides for 6 minutes
Inputs
The Presentation Story in your outline doc and your Creative Strategy Framework doc

All samples in this deck are made up.

Step 1 of 6

Set up your deck

  1. Go to slides.google.com and start a blank presentation.
  2. Pick a simple theme with strong contrast and name the file.
  3. Add one slide for each row of your slide plan.
Sample (made up)

8 slides. Title slide layout for slide 1, Title only or Title and body for the rest.

You're on track if… Your slide count matches your Presentation Story.

If you're stuck… Not sure which theme? Simple Light or Simple Dark both work. You can add Beats red later.

Step 2 of 6

Type all your action titles first

Paste each action title into its slide's title box before you add anything else.

Sample (made up)

Slide 3: Stars get attention, not trust · Slide 5: Rivals are catching up with creators · Slide 8: Next step: test one star-plus-fan TikTok against a star-only version

You're on track if… In View, then Grid view, your titles read like your story.

If you're stuck… A title won't fit on one or two lines? Cut it to the point.

Step 3 of 6

Add evidence to each insight slide

Add 2–3 short bullets or one simple chart. Put the source in small text under it.

Sample (made up)

Slide 3: a 2-bar chart, Attention 4.5 and Believable 3.0, plus one line: "8 of 12 said it felt like an ad." Source: Project 2 survey, 12 responses.

You're on track if… Each insight slide has a number and says where it came from.

If you're stuck… Use your rubric scores from your Framework doc. Example: Beats_YouTube_01 scored 19 of 25 but failed on Feels real (2).

Step 4 of 6

Build your Application and creative sample

Your recommendation says which insight it comes from. The creative sample is optional, and 3 frames is enough.

Sample (made up)

Slide 6: Pair a star with a real fan in the first 3 seconds (from Insight 1). Slide 7: Frame 1, a real fan mid-run as the music drops. Frame 2, the star joins and Beats is on screen by 5 seconds. Frame 3, the fan says why they wear them.

You're on track if… Someone could sketch the ad from your slide.

If you're stuck… Can't draw? Use 3 boxes with a one-line caption each, or a Canva frame.

Step 5 of 6

Put your talk track in the speaker notes

Copy the "What I'll say" lines from your Presentation Story into the notes box under each slide.

Sample (made up)

Notes, slide 5: "In my repository, Skullcandy's ads scored 4.1 on average, almost the same as Beats at 4.2. In my survey, 6 of 12 people picked a Skullcandy creator ad as the most believable."

You're on track if… Every slide has 1–3 lines of notes.

If you're stuck… Don't see the notes box? Choose View, then Show speaker notes.

Step 6 of 6

Check it's readable, then share it

  1. Body text 24pt or larger, titles 36pt or larger.
  2. Alt text on every image and chart. No meaning shown by color alone.
  3. Click Share. Under General access, choose Anyone with the link, Viewer.

You're on track if… A friend can read every slide on a phone screen.

If you're stuck… A slide feels crowded? Move a bullet into the speaker notes.

How it will be evaluated

What a strong deck looks like

Structure
6–10 slides that follow Context, Key Insights, Application
Action titles
Every slide title states a point
Evidence
Each insight has a number with its source, matching your own work
Application
A clear recommendation tied to an insight and one next step; optional creative sample
Readable
Big text, strong contrast, alt text, speaker notes, link set to view
