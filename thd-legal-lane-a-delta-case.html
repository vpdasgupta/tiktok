Real example Surveillance pricing

A price built just for you?

In 2025, Delta Air Lines said it wanted to use AI to help set ticket prices, on roughly 1 in 5 of its U.S. flights by year’s end. The worry: instead of one price everyone sees, the price could be aimed at you.

🛫 Same seat, different price, depending on who’s buying

Real example The pushback

Congress noticed.

In July 2025, three U.S. Senators sent Delta a letter. Their fear: AI could charge each traveler right up to their “pain point,” the most they’d pay before walking away. Delta pushed back, saying it does not use personal data to target individuals.

📌 “Pain point” = the most you’d pay before saying no

Real example Why it matters for the law

New tech pulls the law toward it.

No law bans this yet. But this is exactly how it starts: lawmakers test whether today’s privacy and consumer rules are enough. That pressure is part of what’s driving the pricing‑disclosure bills you’ll meet next.
