Six Minutes, Two or Three Insights

How to shape your final presentation · 7 slides · use Next or swipe

Your recorded deliverable

Your final presentation answers three questions

6 minutes

  • 1 · What did you research?A brief overview of your approach
  • 2 · What are your key insights?Your 2–3 most important findings
  • 3 · How could Beats apply this?Ideas Beats can use, with an optional creative sample

Audience: Beats leadership. You start from two things you already have: your Final Presentation Outline and your Creative Strategy Framework doc.

Why 6 minutes

A short slot forces you to choose

Executives have limited time. Six minutes makes you decide what matters most.

  • Prioritize hardKeep only what Beats needs to hear
  • Speak plainlyNo jargon and no filler
  • Respect your audienceLeadership's time is the scarcest thing in the room
  • Find the signalThe limit shows you which findings stand out from the noise

The structure

Three parts fill your six minutes

Part 1 · Context
1 minute
Who you are, what you researched and why it matters to Beats
Part 2 · Key insights
3–4 minutes
Your 2–3 findings. For each one, say what you found, why it matters and your evidence. This is the heart of your presentation.
Part 3 · Application
1–2 minutes
How Beats can use your findings, an optional creative sample and one clear next step

Choosing your insights

Keep the findings that pass four tests

Pick 2–3

  • SurprisingAn unexpected finding grabs attention
  • ActionableBeats can do something with it
  • Evidence-basedYou have proof from your research
  • Beats-specificA generic insight adds no value

You'll have more than three good findings. Present the 2–3 that pass the most tests.

Weak vs. strong

A strong insight is specific to Beats

Weak insight

“Short-form video is important for Gen Z.” Everyone already knows this, and it says nothing about Beats.

Strong insight (example)

“Stars get attention, not trust. Beats_YouTube_01 scored 4.5 on attention but 3.0 on believable, and 8 of 12 people said it felt like an ad.” It's specific, backed by evidence and about Beats.

The numbers in this example are made up. Your insights come from your own Ad Repository, survey and framework.

Extras

A sample shows it. A coach sharpens it.

Optional creative sample

Show how Beats could use your insight: a simple storyboard, a written 15–30 second video concept, a mood board, or a real Beats ad that shows your principles.

AI as your presentation coach

Ask Claude or ChatGPT to review your framework for the strongest insights, shape your story, tighten your language and predict leadership's questions.

You make the final calls. AI can suggest. You decide what goes in.

What good looks like

Strong presentations do seven things

  • Open with confidenceSet the context in your first minute
  • Share 2–3 insightsNot everything you found
  • Give specific evidenceFor every insight
  • Tie it to BeatsConnect each point to Beats' business needs
  • End with a clear call to actionTell leadership what to do next
  • Finish within 6 minutesPractice out loud and time yourself
  • Use visuals that support youSlides back up what you say
