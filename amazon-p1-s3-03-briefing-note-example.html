Part 1 of 6Title: who, what, where

Early-Tenure Attrition Among New Associates at an Illustrative Amazon FC

Part 2 of 6Set the scene

Context

Amazon employed about 1,576,000 people at the end of 2025 (Form 10-K, FY2025), but it doesn't publish a turnover rate. This note models an illustrative fulfillment center of 100 associates, using public estimates.

Part 3 of 6Numbers, not feelings

Problem Statement

New associates appear to leave very early. Leaked Amazon research reported in 2022 found that only about one in three people hired in 2021 stayed 90 days (Engadget, 2022). A 2021 New York Times investigation, using pre-2020 data, put annual turnover for hourly workers at about 150%. Amazon hasn't confirmed either figure. For comparison, the whole transportation and warehousing sector had a quits rate of 2.2% a month in 2025, roughly 26% a year (BLS JOLTS).

Part 4 of 6Tie it to business impact

Why It Matters

At 124% to 150% turnover (NELP, 2022; New York Times, 2021), our illustrative site would replace 124 to 150 associates a year. At $4,700 to $8,000 per replacement (SHRM, 2022; Center for American Progress, 2012), that costs between about $583,000 and $1.2 million a year. At 150%, about $800,000 of the mid estimate comes from people who leave in their first 90 days. Gallup found that 52% of people who quit say their employer could have done something to keep them (2019), so much of this cost may be preventable.

Part 5 of 6Drivers, not proof

Root Causes (preliminary)

Pay is probably not the main driver: Amazon raised its minimum starting wage to $20 an hour in 2026. Public employee reviews point to other causes: "constant pressure to maintain exhausting rate," communication that is "poorly handled leading to inconsistent work practices," and having to rely on a chatbot for help. These are early signals from a few reviews, not proven causes.

Part 6 of 6What to investigate

Next Steps

We recommend (1) analyzing a larger set of employee reviews to see which themes appear most among people who left in their first year (the closest the review data gets to the 90-day window), (2) comparing what new and tenured associates say about managers and communication, and (3) checking whether new hires know about existing benefits such as Career Choice, which only 11% of eligible associates used in 2025.

Notice: every number has a source, causes are labeled as early signals, and the note ends with questions to investigate, not a fix.
