Where labels get hit: the 5 damage sources

Select a marker on the scene to read what threatens a label there. Everything on the scene is also written out in the list below it.

Read all five damage sources as a list
  • 1. Forklift strikes — Forks and pallets swing wide when lifting product on and off racking. Anything on the beam in the path gets clipped — the single biggest threat.
  • 2. Ladder contact — Associates lean ladders against beams to reach high stock, scraping whatever sits on the shelf edge.
  • 3. Pallet drops — Heavy pallets set down or slid into place can crush or shear a label below or beside them.
  • 4. High-touch bays — Busy, hands-on areas (lumber, fasteners, promo endcaps) where product is constantly pulled and restocked — lots of accidental contact.
  • 5. Outdoor / weather exposure — Garden, lumber yards, and covered outdoor areas add heat, cold, rain, and dust that stress a screen and its battery.

Reading the outcome

Meaning is shown by the word and the shape, not by color.

▲High Flush + rugged casing + rated display. Survives forklift zones (or is cheap enough to replace).
■Medium Some protection but a real weakness. Fine for low-traffic bays, risky in the hardest ones.
▼Low Protrudes, fragile, or unrated. Likely to repeat the failed whole-store test.

Rate against the hardest bay you'd realistically place it in, and always write one sentence saying why.
