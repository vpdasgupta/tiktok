Anatomy of the Technology Assessment Report

One report, three parts, about 2–3 pages. Study the shape and depth — not the specific findings.

What it contains

Chroma scored on cost, durability, and functionality against one market-leader benchmark (VusionGroup), with damage resistance weighted heaviest, on a simple High / Med / Low scale. Fold in a line on how Chroma has performed for HD.

What makes it credible

A Low on damage resistance caps the ranking regardless of cost or features. Thin cells are marked ‘unverified — gap’ rather than guessed.

What it contains

Which ESL form factors present the highest and lowest damage risk in forklift-traffic areas — flush/cased/e-ink is lowest risk, protruding/fragile is highest.

What makes it credible

It treats damage risk as a property of a form factor placed in a bay, not of a brand — so the recommendation can specify where Chroma works.

What it contains

Keep / expand Chroma vs. reconsider, in 2–3 sentences, plus the 2–3 biggest gaps you’d still need to close (e.g. verified cost, warranty & rugged mounting, real damage rates).

What makes it credible

It shows calibrated confidence — a clear keep-or-reconsider call, names an alternative only as a conditional fallback, stays selective, and is openly provisional.

Top to bottom, this is the whole report — a scorecard, a risk read, and a call.
