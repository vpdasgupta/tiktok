The two kinds of fix

SAY fix and DO fix

Most problems need both.

  • SAY fixWhat The Home Depot tells people: shoppers, associates, its own leaders or a regulator.
  • DO fixWhat The Home Depot changes: a step, a setting, a contract or how long it keeps data.

BIPA itself asks for both: tell people in writing before a face scan (SAY), then get a signed release and follow a written schedule for destroying the data (DO). [740 ILCS 14, Section 15]

Worked example

One #1 problem, two fixes

BIPA

#1 problem
Self-checkout cameras may scan shoppers' faces without written consent.
SAY fix
Show a plain-language notice at Illinois self-checkouts saying what the camera collects and why.
DO fix
Get written consent before any Illinois self-checkout camera identifies a face, or turn face-matching off until a consent step exists.

Better: closer to the person and the vendor

Better

SAY
Put that notice right on the self-checkout screen, not only in a policy online.
DO
Also require the camera vendor's contract to follow the same consent and delete rules.

Best: ready for the next state

Best

SAY
Also brief Home Depot's leaders now, so a similar law in another state isn't a surprise.
DO
Use one consent standard in every state, not just Illinois.

Your call

Which fix leads?

  • Lead with DOwhen the problem may be happening right now.
  • Lead with SAYwhen telling people is most of what the law requires, or a change has to wait for a guess to be confirmed.

Example: "I'd lead with the DO fix because the cameras may be scanning faces today."
