Hook anatomy

The 3-second rule

Second 1Is this worth looking at? (the image)
Second 2What is this about? (the words, spoken or on screen)
Second 3Will this be worth my time? (the promise)

A hook answers all three before the thumb moves.

Every hook has three parts

👁️Visual

What's on screen in second 1.

💬Text or audio

What it's about, on screen and said out loud.

➡️Promise

The reason to keep watching.

When all three point the same way, the hook feels obvious.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with the brand name ("Breaking Games presents...").
  • Too much setup.
  • Too generic ("Check out this game!").
  • Hype ("The BEST game EVER").
  • A spec sheet first: players, ages and play time before any fun.
  • A logo, price or promo code in the first 3 seconds.
  • No visual hook, so sound-off viewers miss it.

On-voice, not edgy

Breaking Games sounds like a charismatic emcee, not a scene-stealing performer. Show the game before the punchline.

✅ On-voice

"This just in: this game ended our group chat argument." The cards are in frame first, and an old newscast line is the hook.

🚫 Edgy (out)

Any joke at a person's or a group's expense, or anything just for shock.

Occasionally ridiculous, not unhinged: a pun is a flavor, not the main dish.

The Op case: real play beats polish

The Op, another game company, got about 8 million TikTok views in one month. Most viewers didn't follow the account.

  • Low-polish phone footage of real play, not studio ads.
  • About 80% entertaining posts to 20% promoting.
  • For you: film real hands at a real table, and entertain first. Promote rarely.
