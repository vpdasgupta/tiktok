Technology Assessment Report — Chroma Labels

For the Home Depot Merchandising Team · Jordan Rivera

1. My Recommendation

Keep Chroma, but only test it in a few quiet, low-traffic spots — not the whole store yet.

Chroma saves the team a lot of time. Right now workers change price labels by hand, about 5 seconds each, thousands of times a week, across 2,000+ stores. A digital label changes the price on its own. That is a real win.

But Home Depot already tried these labels store-wide once, and they broke. So I would start small — put them only where things don't get bumped or hit — and grow from there once we answer the questions below.

Before we do more, we still need to find out:

  • Still to confirm How much each Chroma label actually costs.
  • Still to confirm Whether Chroma sells a tougher version that can handle bumps, dust, and water.
  • Still to confirm How often Chroma labels break in a real, busy store.

2. Is Chroma a Good Fit?

I looked at three things. The most important one is how well it survives damage.

  • Toughness (most important): Okay, not great. It should be fine in calm spots, but a normal label sticks out where forklifts and carts can hit it. Still to confirm
  • Cost: Don't know yet. I couldn't find a real price, so I can't say if it's worth it. Still to confirm
  • What it does: Strong. It changes prices for you and saves the team hours of work.

3. How Chroma Compares to Hanshow

I compared Chroma to Hanshow, one of the biggest label makers. I picked Hanshow because it makes a tougher label built to take hits — which is exactly Home Depot's problem.

4. What Warehouse Clubs Did

Home Depot's stores work like warehouses, so I looked at how warehouse clubs — Costco, Sam's Club, and BJ's Wholesale — use digital labels. They have the same problem: forklifts, big pallets, and busy aisles.

How warehouse clubs use digital labels
Club What they did What Home Depot can learn
BJ's Wholesale Put digital labels in every store, chain-wide, and says price changes are much faster now. It can work at full scale in a club store — and the labels sit on a protected rail, not sticking out.
Sam's Club Owned by Walmart, which is a big digital-label user; rolling them out in club aisles. Still to confirm A close cousin worth watching — but I couldn't confirm exactly how far along they are.
Costco Couldn't confirm that Costco uses digital shelf labels at scale. Still to confirm Not every warehouse retailer has jumped in — so this isn't a "must do," it's a choice.

What HD should borrow: BJ's mounts its labels on a protected rail so they don't stick out into the aisle. That's the single most useful idea here — Home Depot should copy that rail mounting for its forklift lanes before expanding.
