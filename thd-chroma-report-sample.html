Technology Assessment Report — Chroma ESL

Prepared for the Merchandising Execution Team (MET) · Jordan Rivera · Discover Phase, Project 1

1.Recommendation & Top Gaps

Keep Chroma — but hold any expansion to a small, low-damage pilot until two gaps close. Chroma clears the durability bar in quiet, low-traffic bays (paint samples, back-wall shelving), and on labor savings the case is strong: MET changes labels by hand at roughly 5 seconds each across 2,000+ stores. But its sealing, rugged-mount options, and real warehouse failure rates are unconfirmed — and "Chroma everywhere" is exactly what sank Home Depot's last whole-store ESL rollout on damage and malfunctions. Pilot it where the bay is forgiving, not in the forklift lanes, and reassess once the vendor answers the gaps below.

The 2–3 gaps that decide expansion

  • Unverified — gap Verified Chroma cost. We have no confirmed per-label list price or volume pricing. A distributor quote is needed before any bay-count math holds up.
  • Unverified — gap Rugged / recessed mounting & sealing. We could not confirm whether Chroma offers a flush or recessed housing or an IP-sealed variant for dust-and-moisture zones like the garden bays.
  • Unverified — gap Real warehouse damage rates. Documented Chroma failure rates in high-traffic retail are not public — this needs a direct vendor conversation, not a spec sheet.

4.Damage-Risk Snapshot

A smart label is only as tough as its weakest element in a given bay — the bay decides, not the brochure. Read by the four physical elements against Home Depot's real warehouse threats: forklift and pallet-jack lanes, falling lumber and heavy stock, customer and associate contact during restock, on-shelf malfunctions, and outdoor exposure in the garden bays.

Note on specs: no IP or impact (IK) rating was published for Chroma, and a missing rating is a gap, not a pass. Figures like "IP65," "400-nit display," or "5-year battery" are what a confirmed spec would look like — none was verified for Chroma, so each is left open above.
