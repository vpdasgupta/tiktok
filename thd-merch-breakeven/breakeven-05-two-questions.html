The date slips later — fast. Ongoing cost eats into your monthly savings, so net shrinks and payback stretches. Push ongoing cost to $100/month and net becomes $100, so $2,400 ÷ $100 = 24 months — nine months later than the original 15. Drag ongoing cost up to $200 and it never breaks even at all, because you're spending everything you save. This is exactly why the warehouse damage rate can sink the business case.

The date moves sooner, in a straight line. Net savings don't change, so breakeven is just upfront ÷ net. Cut upfront to $1,600 (keeping net at $160) and $1,600 ÷ $160 = 10 months — five months sooner. Every dollar shaved off hardware and install pulls the breakeven date directly forward, which is why those prices are worth negotiating hard.

The big idea: breakeven isn't one fixed answer — it's a date that moves the moment any assumption changes. Your real job in Project 2 is to show, honestly, what makes that date move earlier or later.
