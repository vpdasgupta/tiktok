3.2 Build Your Results Tab

Add a Results tab to your response Sheet

Your Google Form already sends every response to a Sheet. You'll add 2 tabs to it: Clean and Results.

  • Ad averagesOverall + the 4 grid rows, per ad
  • Beats' best and worstIts strongest and weakest attribute
  • Compare winnersVotes for each compare question
  • Coded answersEach written answer with a code
  • 6 quotesThe ones you'll use later
  • Sample lineHow many responses you kept

All sample numbers in this deck are made up.

Step 1 of 6

Make a Clean tab

  1. In your Form, open Responses and click View in Sheets.
  2. Right-click the Form Responses 1 tab and choose Duplicate. Rename the copy Clean.
  3. On Clean, delete rows that are incomplete, failed the attention check, or straight-lined.
  4. Add a Results tab. In cell A1, type your Sample line.
Sample (made up)

Sample: 12 responses kept (14 in, 1 failed the attention check, 1 straight-lined). Collected over 4 days.

You're on track if… Form Responses 1 is untouched, and Clean has only the rows you trust.

If you're stuck… Don't delete rows on Form Responses 1. Your Form keeps writing to it, so work on the copy.

Step 2 of 6

Build your ad averages table

One row per ad. Each cell: =ROUND(AVERAGE(Clean!C2:C13),1) for that column.

Example (made up): average scores, 1–5, from 12 responses
AdOverallAttentionMemorableBelievableLearn more
Beats_YouTube_014.14.54.23.03.4
Beats_TikTok_013.83.93.53.93.6
Skullcandy_TikTok_013.93.83.44.23.7
Sony_YouTube_013.33.03.13.83.2

You're on track if… Every cell is between 1 and 5.

If you're stuck… #DIV/0! means an empty range. Check the column letter.

Step 3 of 6

Name Beats' strongest and weakest attribute

Under the table, write 2 lines. Use the gap rule: under 0.2 is a tie, 0.2–0.5 is notable, over 0.5 is clear.

Sample (made up)
Beats' strongest
Attention on Beats_YouTube_01: 4.5. Clear lead over every other ad.
Beats' weakest
Believable on Beats_YouTube_01: 3.0. 1.2 below Skullcandy_TikTok_01, a clear gap.

You're on track if… Each line names the ad, the attribute, the score and a comparison.

If you're stuck… Two Beats ads? Pick the attribute where Beats is furthest above or below the rivals.

Step 4 of 6

Count the compare winners

Ads down the side, compare questions across the top. Each cell: =COUNTIF(Clean!R2:R13,"Beats_YouTube_01").

Example (made up): compare-question votes, out of 12
AdMost memorableMost believableMost want to buy
Beats_YouTube_01714
Beats_TikTok_01232
Skullcandy_TikTok_01265
Sony_YouTube_01121

You're on track if… Each column adds up to your kept responses.

If you're stuck… All zeros? The ad name must match the Form option exactly.

Step 5 of 6

Code the written answers

Add 3 headers below your tables: Ad ID, Answer, Code. Paste every written answer from Clean, one per row, and give each a code from your list of 5–8.

Sample (made up): Ad ID · Answer · Code

Beats_YouTube_01 · "Cool, but I'd never buy headphones because of that." · Feels like an ad

You're on track if… Every answer has one code. =COUNTIFS can count each code per ad.

If you're stuck… Too many codes? Merge ones that mean the same thing.

Step 6 of 6

Pick 6 quotes

Add a Quotes section with 3 headers: Ad ID, Quote, Code. Pick 6 quotes. Cover all 4 ads, and include some good and some bad.

Sample (made up)
Beats_YouTube_01
"Cool, but I'd never buy headphones because of that." (Feels like an ad)
Skullcandy_TikTok_01
"It felt like a friend showing me something." (Trusts the person)

You're on track if… Each quote is short, exact and sounds like its code.

If you're stuck… Not enough answers for one ad? Use 2 from another ad and say so in a note.

How it will be evaluated

What a strong Results tab has

Clean data
A Sample line and a Clean tab with bad responses removed. No time filter.
Averages
Overall and all 4 grid rows for all 4 ads
Beats' best and worst
Named with a number and a comparison, using the gap rule
Compare winners
Vote counts for all 3 compare questions
Codes and quotes
Every written answer coded with 5–8 codes, plus 6 exact quotes
