Turn Your Outline into a Presentation Story

Step 1 of 6

Add the section and pick your insights

  1. Under Project Notes, add a heading: Presentation Story.
  2. Check each Key Insight: surprising, actionable, evidence-based, Beats-specific.
  3. Keep the 2–3 that pass the most tests.
Sample (made up)

Keep Insight 1, Stars get attention, not trust (passes all 4). Keep Insight 2, Rivals are catching up with creators. Cut Tech facts are trusted but forgotten: it doesn't lead to my Application.

You're on track if… Every insight you keep has at least one number behind it.

If you're stuck… Rank your insights by which one Beats could act on next week.

Step 2 of 6

Write your big idea in one sentence

This is the answer your whole presentation holds up. It goes right under the Presentation Story heading.

Sample (made up)

Big idea: Beats ads get noticed. Pairing stars with real fans can get them believed.

You're on track if… It answers "So what should Beats do?"

If you're stuck… Join your strongest insight to your #1 recommendation with "so".

Step 3 of 6

Write an action title for each slide

Plan 6–10 slides: a title slide, about 1 for Context, 1–2 per insight, 1–2 for Application and a close.

Sample (made up)
1–2
Stars get Beats noticed. Real fans get Beats believed. · I studied 25 ads and asked 12 people what they believed
3–5
Stars get attention, not trust · My rubric flags the same gap · Rivals are catching up with creators
6–8
Pair a star with a real fan in the first 3 seconds · What it could look like · Next step: test one star-plus-fan TikTok against a star-only version

You're on track if… Your titles, read alone, tell the story.

If you're stuck… A title over 10 words usually holds two ideas. Split it.

Step 4 of 6

Add what you'll say and your evidence

For each slide, write 1–3 short lines you'll say out loud. Name the number and where it came from.

Sample (made up)

Slide 3 · Stars get attention, not trust. "Our athlete ad got the most attention of the 4 ads I tested, 4.5 out of 5. It was also the least believable, 3.0. 8 of 12 people said it felt like an ad." (Project 2 survey)

You're on track if… Every number matches your Ad Repository, Findings tab or Framework doc.

If you're stuck… Need more proof? Use your rubric scores in the Creative Rubric section of your Framework doc.

Step 5 of 6

Time each slide so it fits 6 minutes

Add a time to each row, then add them up. Read your lines out loud with a timer to check.

Sample (made up)

Context 1:00 (slides 1–2) · Key Insights 3:00 (slides 3–5) · Application 1:30 (slides 6–7) · Next step and close 0:30 (slide 8) · Total 6:00

You're on track if… Your total is 6:00 or less and your insights get 3–4 minutes.

If you're stuck… Over time? Cut a slide before you cut words. Most people speak about 130–150 words a minute.

Step 6 of 6

Pressure-test your story with AI

Paste your Presentation Story into Claude or ChatGPT with this prompt. You make the final calls.

Prompt to copy

"I'm giving a 6-minute presentation to Beats by Dre leadership. Here is my slide plan: [paste]. For each slide, is the title a clear point? Is my evidence enough? What would leadership ask? Tell me what to cut to stay under 6 minutes. Don't add insights or facts I haven't given you."

You're on track if… You changed at least one title or cut one thing yourself.

If you're stuck… AI suggests a stat you don't have? Leave it out. You'll be asked about every number.

How it will be evaluated

What a strong presentation story looks like

Big idea
One sentence that answers "so what should Beats do?"
Insights
2–3 that pass the four tests, each with evidence
Action titles
6–10 titles that tell the story when read alone
What you'll say
Short lines with numbers that match your own work
Timing
6:00 or less; Context about 1, Insights 3–4, Application 1–2 minutes
