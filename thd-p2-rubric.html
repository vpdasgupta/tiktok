How Your Breakeven Analysis Is Graded

Three things matter, each worth part of your grade. You're scored on how clearly you think and how well you explain your numbers — not on a fancy spreadsheet or one “right” answer.

Project 2 Breakeven Analysis scoring rubric, with three criteria scored from Exceeds (five stars) to Needs Work (one star).
Criterion & weight Exceeds ★★★★★ Needs Work ★
Evidence Rigor 40% of your score Exceeds ★★★★★ You reach a real payback number — how many months until ESL pays for itself — and every figure has a source. You carry the labor savings you found in Pre-Work into the model as the savings side. Each number ties back to a locked MET fact, a Home Depot number, a benchmark you cite, or an assumption you label clearly. Needs Work ★ You never reach a payback number. Figures show up with no source. Vendor marketing is treated as fact. The labor savings is never measured, or it isn't connected to the model.
THD Fit & Specificity 30% of your score Exceeds ★★★★★ You use Home Depot's higher warehouse damage rates (5/10/15/25%) and the cost of replacing damaged units — not the 2–5% rates from regular stores. You also say what it would take to actually test ESL on specific products or aisles at Home Depot. Needs Work ★ It reads like a generic store, not Home Depot. You ignore forklift zones, locked cages, and the whole-store test that failed. You use average damage rates with no warehouse adjustment. You don't say what it would take to put ESL in place.
AI Integration + Human Judgment 30% of your score Exceeds ★★★★★ You use AI (like Claude) to work faster — setting up assumptions, checking math, testing scenarios — and you show where you checked, fixed, or overruled it against a real source. The thinking is clearly yours. Needs Work ★ You paste AI output in as-is. Made-up specs, rates, or costs are shown as fact. There are no notes on what you checked. Nothing shows you reviewed or corrected the AI.

Showing your work IS the grade. Say two externs turn in the same payback number. One writes “12 months (source: my assumptions).” The other writes “about 12 months, assuming 15% damage — here are my three inputs, where each came from, and where I overruled the AI's guess.” The second extern scores much higher on every part, with the same math.
