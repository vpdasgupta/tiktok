Project 1 — Focus Brief

Buy-Online / In-Aisle Assistance

Prepared by: A. Sample · Lane: Buy-online, pick-up & help in-aisle

My lane is buy-online / in-aisle assistance, and the aspect I'm following is the handoff between a customer's online order and the associate standing next to them in the aisle. I'm following Marcus, a repeat Pro contractor. The friction I've picked most directly moves sales conversion — orders started online that stall in the store.

Part A — Where You'll Focus

The person

Marcus is a small-crew contractor who buys in bulk 2–3 times a week. He starts an order on his phone the night before, then finishes it in-store on the way to a job site.

The current journey

Marcus's trip today, step by step
StepWhat happens
1Builds a cart of 14 items in the app the night before.
2Arrives, needs to confirm 3 items are in stock in this store.
3Finds an associate in the aisle — but the associate can't see Marcus's online cart.
4Re-explains the whole order verbally; two items get missed.
5Walks to a fixed register to check out; the online cart doesn't carry over.

The one friction worth solving

The online cart doesn't follow Marcus into the aisle. The associate helping him can't see what he already picked, so the order is rebuilt by hand at a fixed register — and items fall off along the way.

My defense (two sentences)

This is the point where a started sale is most likely to shrink or stall, because it forces a manual re-do at the exact moment the customer is ready to buy. Fixing the aisle handoff protects an order that the customer has already committed to — which is why I tied it to sales conversion rather than general satisfaction.

How mobile POS relates

A mobile point-of-sale device the associate carries in the aisle is the most direct lever on this friction: it could pull up Marcus's online cart on the spot and close the sale where he's standing, instead of at a fixed register.

The honest split

What I can prove (sourced)

  • Home Depot's app supports building a cart before arriving (product help pages).
  • Associates today carry the hdPhone, but it's used for scanning and stock lookups — not for viewing a customer's cart (deployment notes).

What I'm guessing

  • That items get "missed" during the verbal re-do — likely, but I have no abandonment number for this store. guess
  • That most Pro customers start online the way Marcus does. guess

What I'd check next

  • Ask a store lead how often an aisle associate is asked to look up an online order.
  • Find out whether the hdPhone can technically surface a customer's cart, or if that's a policy limit.
