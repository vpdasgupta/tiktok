Evidence Beats Opinion

Why creative strategists ask real people · 6 slides · use Next or swipe

Where Project 1 left you

You know what ads exist. You don't yet know what works.

Your Ad Repository holds 25–30 Beats and competitor ads. It shows what brands are putting out there.

It can't tell you whether people like those ads, what makes them stop scrolling, or which messages stick.

The big idea: the best creative strategies are built on evidence. In Project 2 you get that evidence from the people the ads are made for.

Opinion vs. evidence

“They said” beats “I think”

Opinion

“I think the athlete ads will land best with college students.” It might be right, but nobody can check it.

Evidence

“7 of 12 people picked the athlete ad as the most memorable. Only 1 of 12 picked it as the most believable.” Now the team can test it and act on it.

These numbers are a made-up example to show what an evidence-based claim looks like. They are not real data.

Evidence lets you defend a recommendation and predict what will connect before Beats spends a production budget.

Your method

You'll ask real people with a short survey

Main: a 4-ad survey

You pick 4 ads from your Ad Repository: 2 Beats and 2 competitors. You build a Google Form, test it, then get at least 10 responses. Strength: answers you can count and compare. Blind spot: people only answer what you asked.

Optional: a quick comment check

You read 15–20 top comments on 2 of the ads you tested. Strength: words people chose on their own. Blind spot: the loudest voices may not be typical.

The survey is the core. The comment check is a bonus that can back up or challenge what you found.

From results to findings

A number plus a quote makes a finding

  • The number shows what happenedOne ad scored highest for grabbing attention but lowest for being believable.
  • The quote shows whyPeople's own words from your open-ended question explain the score.

You'll write 3 findings. With fewer than 30 responses, call them early signals.

How it connects

Each project builds on the one before

You are in Project 2

Project 1: Do
Your competitive analysis showed what Beats and its rivals do in their ads
Project 2: Works
Your consumer research shows what works with real people
Project 3: Should
Your strategic framework sets out what Beats should do next
Project 4: Present
You present your work to Beats leadership in 6 minutes

Without Project 2, your Project 3 recommendations would rest on guesses.

What you'll deliver

You'll hand Beats a 1-page Consumer Feedback Report

  • What I testedHow many people answered, the 4 ads, the dates and 1 limitation
  • 3 findingsA bold line for each, backed by a number and a quote
  • Beats could…2 or 3 ideas the Beats ad team could try next

Every recommendation should point back to something consumers told you.
