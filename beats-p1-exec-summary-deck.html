Write Your Executive Summary

7 steps · 9 slides · use Next or swipe

3.3 Submit your Executive Summary

One Google Doc, 2 pages, 6 sections

Your reader is a Beats creative lead with 5 minutes. Make every line count.

  1. 1 · Summary100–150 words
  2. 2 · Methodology3–4 lines on how you did it
  3. 3 · Key FindingsAbout 1 page
  4. 4 · Competitive PositioningAbout a third of a page
  5. 5 · Strategic Implications2–3 bullets
  6. 6 · AppendixYour sheet link

Write in this order: 3, 4, 5, 2, then 1. The summary is easiest once everything else is done.

Step 1 of 7

Set up your doc

Create a Google Doc. Add a title, your name, the date and the 6 section headings.

Sample

Beats by Dre: Video Ad Landscape

[Your Name] · [Date]

1. Summary · 2. Methodology · 3. Key Findings · 4. Competitive Positioning · 5. Strategic Implications · 6. Appendix

You're on track if… Your doc has a title and all 6 headings, set as Heading 2 so they show in the outline.

If you're stuck… Select a heading, then choose Heading 2 from the style menu on the toolbar.

Step 2 of 7

Section 3: Key Findings

Paste in your 4–6 findings from 3.1 under 4 subheadings: Hooks, Talent and Production, Platforms, Standout Ads. Add one table copied from a pivot tab.

Sample

Hooks (made-up numbers)

Beats opened 6 of its 10 ads with a famous face, vs. 2 of 15 competitor ads. Beats sells who wears the headphones, while rivals lead with the product.

You're on track if… Every finding has a number, a comparison and a so what, and your table matches your sheet.

If you're stuck… Use the finding formula from 3.1 (number + comparison + so what) and fix one sentence at a time.

Step 3 of 7

Section 4: Competitive Positioning

Add your 2×2 map from 3.2. Under it, write one sentence on where Beats sits, then your threat and your opening.

Sample (made up)

Beats sits alone in the premium, style-and-culture box.

Threat: Skullcandy's creator ads score nearly as high, at a lower price.

Opening: No brand in my sample used Question hooks.

You're on track if… A reader can find Beats on your map and name its closest rival.

If you're stuck… Copy the 2×2 table from your 3.2 notes and add a one-line caption.

Step 4 of 7

Section 5: Strategic Implications

Write 2–3 bullets. Each one links a finding to a direction Beats could take. Start each with "Beats could…"

Sample
  • Beats could test Question hooks on TikTok, since no competitor in my sample uses them.
  • Beats could film its celebrities in a creator style so the ads feel at home on TikTok.

You're on track if… Each bullet points back to a finding in Section 3 or 4.

If you're stuck… Turn your opening from 3.2 into one "Beats could…" sentence.

Step 5 of 7

Section 2: Methodology

In 3–4 lines, say how many ads you logged, which brands and platforms, when, and how you sorted and scored them.

Sample

I logged 25 video ads (10 Beats, 15 from Apple AirPods, Sony, Bose, JBL, Samsung and Skullcandy) from each brand's TikTok, Instagram and YouTube accounts in November 2026. I tagged each ad's hook, talent, CTA, sound and production, then scored it from 1 to 5.

You're on track if… Someone could repeat your research from these lines.

If you're stuck… Your Summary Dashboard has your totals by brand and platform.

Step 6 of 7

Section 1: Summary

Write this last. In 100–150 words, say what you studied, give your 3 biggest findings as bullets, and end with one sentence on your main takeaway.

Sample (made up)

I studied 25 headphone video ads to see how Beats grabs attention compared with its rivals.

  • Beats opens with famous faces three times as often as competitors.
  • Creator-style ads scored as well as studio ads.
  • No brand used Question hooks.

Main takeaway: Beats owns culture, but cheaper creator-led rivals are closing in.

You're on track if… If your reader stops here, they still know what you found and why it matters.

If you're stuck… Pick the 3 findings you'd most want a Beats creative lead to remember.

Step 7 of 7

Section 6: Appendix, then check and submit

  1. Paste your Ad Repository link under Appendix.
  2. Check that the doc is 2 pages or less.
  3. Check that every number matches your sheet.
  4. Set the doc and the sheet to "Anyone with the link can view."
  5. Open both links in a private or incognito window.

You're on track if… Both links open in a private window without asking for access.

If you're stuck… In Google Docs or Sheets: Share > General access > Anyone with the link > Viewer.

How it will be evaluated

What a strong Executive Summary does

Backed by data
Every finding uses a number from your sheet
Goes past description
Each finding says what it means for Beats
Clear positioning
The map, threat and opening make Beats' spot obvious
Easy to read
2 pages or less, clear headings, no typos
Links work
Both open for anyone with the link
