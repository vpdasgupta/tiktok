Why 5%? Home Depot data says about 3% of price-label changes replace damaged labels. Spread over a store, that works out to about 5–8% a year store-wide (best guess: ~35,000–50,000 label spots per store) — confirm with Home Depot. Ceiling fans sit in an indoor, lower-traffic aisle, so 5% is the base case; the test still runs 5/10/15/25%, and 15–25% is the stress range for busy or outdoor zones. A hard plastic smart label won't break the same way, so treat it as a starting point.

Breakeven + Sensitivity Tab — The Damage-Rate Test

Partner data + illustrative

Formula: months to breakeven = $392 upfront ÷ (net savings per year ÷ 12), where net savings = labor saved − software − replacement. If net savings is $0 or less, it never pays back.

Tipping points: at 5% damage, fans pay back only if a change takes more than about 5 seconds per label. Home Depot's 5-second swap sits right on that line. At 30 seconds, damage would need to pass about 50% a year before fans stop paying back.
The lesson: moving damage from 5% to 25% shifts payback by less than two years. Moving from 5 to 30 seconds decides whether it pays back at all. For ceiling fans, how long a change really takes matters more than how often labels break.

The 2-Page Summary, Filled In

Partner data + illustrative

The five required parts, filled with this example. Key findings lead with the lane finding.

1 · Executive overview

For ceiling fans in one store, smart labels pay back in about 27 months if a price change takes 30 seconds per label end to end, and never if it takes only the 5-second swap Home Depot measured. The answer depends on time per change far more than on damage.

2 · Key findings by category

Lane finding (Labor Savings): the true labor prize is about $210 a year per store at 30 seconds, and walking to the bay is the single biggest step (8 of 30 seconds). At 5% damage, ceiling fans pay back only above about 5 seconds per label, so the 5-second swap sits right on the line. Faster-changing categories (600 changes a year) pay back in about 12 months; slow ones (50 a year) never do.

3 · Critical assumptions

From Home Depot data (MET, October 2026): 34 label spots, 300 changes a year, 3 labels per change, $28/hour, 5 s per swap. Labeled assumptions: 30 s end to end, $10 per label, $32 install, $20 gateways, $17 a year software, 5% damage (best guess: about 5–8% a year store-wide). The 30 seconds is the least certain and matters most.

4 · Damage-rate impact

At 30 s, moving damage from 5% to 25% stretches payback from 27 to 47 months, less than two years. At 5 s, no damage rate pays back. Damage would need to pass about 50% a year to sink the 30-second case.

5 · Preliminary deployment criteria

A category is a good ESL candidate when price changes are frequent, each change takes more than about 5 seconds per label end to end, and breakeven stays under about 36 months at 5% damage and still pays back at 25%.

Reminder: ceiling fans are the shared example. Your summary models your own category or zone, cites Home Depot data where it exists, and labels every assumption.
