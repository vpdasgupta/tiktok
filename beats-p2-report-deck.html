Write Your Consumer Feedback Report

Your findings on one page

A 1-page Google Doc that someone at Beats could read in 2 minutes. You'll copy most of it from your Findings tab.

  • What I tested1–2 lines: N, the ads, dates, 1 limitation
  • Headline insight2 sentences
  • 3 findingsA bold line, a number and a quote each
  • Beats could…2–3 lines

Optional: 1 chart. All samples in this deck are made up.

Step 1 of 6

Create your Doc

  1. Create a Google Doc named P2_ConsumerFeedbackReport_<YourName>.
  2. Add 4 headings with Format > Paragraph styles > Heading 2: What I Tested, Headline Insight, Findings, Beats Could…
  3. Open your Findings tab in another browser tab. You'll copy from it.

You're on track if… Your Doc has the right name and 4 headings.

If you're stuck… Use real heading styles, not bold text. Screen readers and the outline panel need them.

Step 2 of 6

Write "What I tested"

1–2 lines. How many people, which ads, when, and 1 limitation.

Sample (made up)

12 people (14 in, 2 dropped) rated 4 ads: 2 from Beats, 1 from Skullcandy and 1 from Sony, Oct 6–9. Most were friends and classmates, so these are early signals.

You're on track if… A reader knows N, the ads, the dates and 1 limitation.

If you're stuck… Pick the limitation that matters most. Usually it's sample size or who you asked.

Step 3 of 6

Add your 3 findings

For each: a bold line, one number and one quote. Copy them from your Findings tab and trim.

Sample (made up)

Stars get attention, not trust. Beats_YouTube_01 scored 4.5 on attention but 3.0 on believable. "Cool, but I'd never buy headphones because of that."

You're on track if… Each finding fits in 2 lines and has a number and a quote.

If you're stuck… One finding has no quote? Keep the number and leave the quote out.

Step 4 of 6

Write your headline insight

Write this after your findings. 2 sentences: the pattern across them, and what it means for Beats.

Sample (made up)

Beats' star-led ad won attention but not trust. The creator-led TikTok felt like a friend talking and got the most "I'd buy" votes.

You're on track if… Someone who reads only this knows your main point.

If you're stuck… Ask: if Beats remembers one thing, what should it be?

Step 5 of 6

Add 2–3 "Beats could…" lines

Copy them from your Findings tab. Each one should tie back to a finding above.

Sample (made up)
  1. Beats could pair the star with a real fan.
  2. Beats could test more creator-led TikToks.
  3. Beats could put one product fact inside a story.

You're on track if… Each line names something you would see in the ad.

If you're stuck… Too vague? Ask what changes on screen.

Step 6 of 6

Optional chart, then fit it on one page

  1. Optional: in your Results tab, select the believable column and choose Insert > Chart. Copy it into your Doc.
  2. Give the chart a title that says the point, like "Believable score by ad (1–5)."
  3. Check File > Print preview. Cut words until it fits on 1 page.
  4. Set sharing to "Anyone with the link can view."

You're on track if… It fits on 1 page and the link opens for anyone.

If you're stuck… Over a page? Cut the chart first, then trim each finding to one line.

Sample

A finished 1-page report (made up)

What I tested

12 people (14 in, 2 dropped) rated 2 Beats ads, 1 Skullcandy ad and 1 Sony ad, Oct 6–9. Most were friends and classmates, so these are early signals.

Headline insight

Beats' star-led ad won attention but not trust. The creator-led TikTok felt like a friend talking and got the most "I'd buy" votes.

3 findings

  1. Stars get attention, not trust. Attention 4.5, believable 3.0. "Cool, but I'd never buy headphones because of that."
  2. Creators earn trust. Skullcandy_TikTok_01: believable 4.2; 5 of 12 would most want to buy. "It felt like a friend showing me something."
  3. Tech facts are trusted but forgotten. Sony_YouTube_01: believable 3.8, memorable 3.1.

Beats could…

  1. pair the star with a real fan
  2. test more creator-led TikToks
  3. put one product fact inside a story
