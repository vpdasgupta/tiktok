3.1 Read Your Survey Results

From raw responses to results in 4 moves

Start this now, while responses are still coming in. You'll use all of it in 3.2.

  • 1 · CleanDrop the responses you can't trust
  • 2 · AverageOne score per ad, per question
  • 3 · CompareBeats vs. rivals, and who won each compare question
  • 4 · CodeSort the written answers and pick quotes

All examples in these slides are made up. They use 4 ads from the Project 1 example repository.

Move 1 · Clean

Drop the responses you can't trust

  • IncompleteThey skipped whole ads or stopped partway.
  • Failed the attention checkThe row said "For this row, pick Agree" and they picked something else.
  • Straight-linersThe same answer on every row, all the way down.

Never filter by how long someone took. Google Forms doesn't record completion time, so there's nothing to filter on.

Example (made up): 14 responses came in. 1 failed the attention check and 1 straight-lined, so 12 are left.

Move 2 · Average

One average per ad, per question

In Sheets, =AVERAGE(C2:C13) gives the average of one column. Do it for the overall rating and each grid row, for every ad.

Move 3 · Compare

Read Beats against its rivals

Look across each Beats ad's row. Find its highest and lowest score. Then look down each column to see who beat Beats.

Move 3 · Compare

The gap rule: is the difference real?

How to read the gap between two averages
GapCall itExample (made up)
Under 0.2A tieOverall: Beats_TikTok_01 3.8 vs. Skullcandy_TikTok_01 3.9
0.2 to 0.5NotableOverall: Beats_YouTube_01 4.1 vs. Beats_TikTok_01 3.8
Over 0.5ClearBelievable: Skullcandy_TikTok_01 4.2 vs. Beats_YouTube_01 3.0

Small sample, careful words. Under 30 responses, call what you see "early signals." Say "in my 12 responses," not "people think."

Move 4 · Code

Code the written answers with 5–8 codes

Read every answer to "What stood out to you, good or bad?" Give each one a short code. Keep your list to 5–8 codes so you can count them.

  • Feels like an ad"Too polished," "just a commercial"
  • Trusts the person"Felt real," "like a friend"
  • Product factMentions battery, sound, fit
  • Music or energyMentions the song or the pace
  • Wants to try it"I'd check these out"
  • ConfusingDidn't get what was sold

Example (made up): 8 of 12 answers about Beats_YouTube_01 got the code "Feels like an ad."

Move 4 · Code

Pick quotes that show a pattern

  • Specific"Cool, but I'd never buy headphones because of that" beats "It was good."
  • TypicalIt sounds like the code it came from, not a one-off.
  • Both sidesKeep some good and some bad. Don't pick only what agrees with you.
  • Exact wordsCopy it as written. Fix nothing but obvious typos.

Example (made up): On Skullcandy_TikTok_01, one person wrote, "It felt like a friend showing me something."

Using AI

Let AI do the math, then check it yourself

You can paste your cleaned columns into an AI tool and ask for the averages and vote counts. Then check its work.

Prompt you can copy

Here are my cleaned survey answers, one row per person. Columns C–G are 1–5 scores for one ad. Give me the average of each column, rounded to 1 decimal, in a table. Don't guess any missing values.

Then check… Run =AVERAGE on 2 of its numbers. If either one is off, redo them all in Sheets.

Watch out… AI can miscount rows or blur ad names. You're the one who signs the findings.
