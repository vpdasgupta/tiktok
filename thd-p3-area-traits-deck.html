Describe your area

Five traits, not a floor plan

MET decides by what the products are like. Score each trait High, Medium or Low, with one line of evidence.

  • 1 · Price volatilityLever 6: price changes
  • 2 · Locked or hard to reachLever 3: seconds per change
  • 3 · Features or comparison shoppingBigger labels, online prices
  • 4 · Bulky trafficLever 4: damage
  • 5 · Wi-Fi reachCan the labels get updates?

Trait 1 of 5

Price volatility

Lever 6

Ask
Do prices follow a market that moves (lumber, copper wire, pipes and fittings), or frequent sales and online price-matching?
Why MET cares
More price changes = more paper changes saved. Lumber was repriced daily at times.

Trait 2 of 5

Locked or hard to reach

Lever 3

Ask
Are products in cages or locked cases, or are labels high up?
Why MET cares
Each paper change takes longer. The locked case at the front of the store already uses Chroma.

Trait 3 of 5

Features or comparison shopping

Ask
Do shoppers compare features and prices online before buying, like appliances?
Why MET cares
A bigger label can show features too, and prices move with online competitors. Appliances already use large-format Chroma.

Trait 4 of 5

Bulky traffic

Lever 4

Ask
Do carts, pallets, forklifts or big boxes pass close to the shelf edge?
Why MET cares
More damage. Taylor: “anything big and bulky… that's the worst areas to be in.”

Trait 5 of 5

Wi-Fi reach

Ask
Is your area in a far corner, outdoors, or behind lots of metal racking?
Why MET cares
The last whole-store test ended mostly because labels in remote areas couldn't be updated.
