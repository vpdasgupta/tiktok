A Framework Turns Taste into a Standard

The big idea for Project 3 · 6 slides · use Next or swipe

Why this project

“I like it” doesn't scale

When one person says an ad feels right, nobody else can repeat that call. A team making Beats ads needs shared criteria to judge creative before it ships.

In Project 1 you collected ads. In Project 2 you heard how real people react to them. Project 3 turns both into something an agency or internal team can use.

The big idea: a framework turns taste into a standard. It writes down what good looks like, so anyone can check their work against it.

What goes in

Your framework stands on three inputs

Platform rules
What YouTube, TikTok and Meta publish about what works on their platforms. You'll check each source with the CRAAP test.
Consumer evidence
What your Project 2 Consumer Feedback Report showed about how people respond to ads.
Competitive landscape
What your Project 1 Ad Repository showed about how Beats and its rivals advertise.

Use all three. Platform rules alone give you generic ads. Your own research is what makes the framework fit Beats.

What comes out

Four documents, four questions

  • Platform Best Practices GuideWhat works on each platform?
  • Beats Brand Creative StandardsWhat makes an ad feel like Beats?
  • Creative Effectiveness RubricHow do you score an ad?
  • Competitive Creative AnalysisWhere can Beats stand apart from AirPods, Sony and Bose?

You'll build one document per step, from Step 2 through Step 5.

The rubric

The rubric is where your framework gets tested

Criteria
What you score. Example: “The product appears in the first 3 seconds.”
Scoring definitions
What a low, middle and high score look like for each criterion, in plain words.
Pass/fail threshold
The score an ad needs before it ships. Example: at least 3 out of 5 on every criterion.

The test: two people score the same ad with your rubric and land close. If their scores are far apart, tighten the definitions and try again.

The one-page summary

AI spots patterns. You decide what stays.

What Claude helps with

Reading your four documents side by side, pointing out ideas that repeat and drafting a first version of the summary.

What you decide

Which patterns your evidence supports, what to cut and which recommendations you would defend in front of Beats.

Your judgment is the deliverable. If you can't explain why a line is in the summary, take it out.
